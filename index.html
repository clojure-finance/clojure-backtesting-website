Open-source backtesting library · v1.1.0

Test trading ideas on history, one day at a time.

Clojure Backtesting replays daily market data, fills your orders the way a broker would, tracks cash, loans and short positions, and reports how the strategy performed.

Get started How it works GitHub
strategy.clj
(load-dataset "resources/sample-data/main" "main" add-aprc)
(init-portfolio "1990-01-02" 10000)

;; Hold 50 shares of AAA while its price
;; is above its 10-day moving average
(while (< (compare (get-date) "1990-03-29") 0)
  (let [price (get-permno-price "10001")
        ma10  (moving-avg "10001" 10)]
    (when (and price ma10)
      (order "10001" (if (> price ma10) 50 0)
             :remaining true)))
  (update-eval-report)
  (next-date))
(end-order)

What you can do

A backtest replays history: your strategy sees the market one day at a time, places orders, and the library keeps the books. These are the parts it handles for you.

Day-by-day simulation

A global clock moves through trading days. Your code only sees data up to "today", so it cannot peek at the future.

Orders fill like a broker's

An order fills at the next trading day's close. Orders wait while a security has no price, and expire after a few days.

Margin and short selling

Borrow cash or shares under initial and maintenance margins, with optional commissions and loan interest.

Dividends, splits, fundamentals

Dividends are reinvested or paid to cash. Company filings are joined by report date, so a strategy only sees published numbers.

Technical indicators

Moving averages, EMA, MACD, RSI, rate of change, parabolic SAR, ATR and Keltner Channels.

Performance metrics

Daily returns, volatility, Sharpe ratio, maximum drawdown and profit per trade, plus line charts in the browser.

Automated rules

Pair a condition with an action, such as a stop-loss, and the backtester checks it every trading day.

Data larger than memory

Data is read from disk one day at a time, with a cache. It has been tested with datasets of up to 30 GB.

How a backtest works

Every backtest follows the same four steps. The Key Concepts page explains each step in more detail.

1

Load the data

load-dataset points the library at daily prices, and optionally at company fundamentals.

2

Start a portfolio

init-portfolio sets the start date and the cash you begin with.

3

Step through the days

Each day, read prices or indicators, call order, then next-date to move on.

4

Review the results

end-order closes the positions. The records and the evaluation report show what happened.

What the example above produced

The strategy at the top of this page runs on the sample dataset that ships with the library. It holds 50 shares of the security AAA (10001) whenever the price closes above its 10-day average, and none otherwise.
